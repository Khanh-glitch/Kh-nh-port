## 0. The brief and the non-negotiables

SHOWTIME is **one personal creative exhibition** connecting:
- Live events and backstage production (GET DOWNTOWN, Một Chút Yêu Thương 3, Bản Giao Hưởng Xanh);
- Published/credited music and performance (vocalist in “Nếu ngày mai”; composer for “Mùa Hạ Thứ 18”; 28 live-stage photos);
- Content, voice, commercial-format videos and experiments (Que Quá Quao, ~1.1M-view specific TikTok post, China Town, museum film, crowd experiment, VEINORA, narration and short-form ads);
- Actual interactive/game/product work (public APEX CHAOS demo, source-backed MathPath, EventFlow rebuilding, **Ragnarok: The Last Island** in development).

The art direction **must not** present these as a visually unrelated resume catalog or force all works into a single gaming, tech or concert aesthetic. Different media retain their own identities.

**Owner's explicit decisions, not negotiable:**
1. No AI-authored HTML, UI mockups, wireframes, 3D world prototypes or site deployment in the current phase.
2. Do **not cut/trim** existing videos/audio or compile short highlight montages. Offer the original work in full when possible. Still photos *may* be curated.
3. Don't generate false footage, gallery screenshots, achievements or performance data.
4. Version labels must reflect the truth: Ragnarok and EventFlow under development; MathPath release-candidate scope rather than paid commercial success.
5. Que Quá Quao is the correct name, and its linked viral post has **approximately 1.1 million owner-reported views**: https://www.tiktok.com/@quequaquao/video/7530989283956559122.
6. The site should let a recruiter see authentic proof quickly AND let interested visitors explore deeply; neither requires playing an exploratory minigame.

## 1. External design research — what is real and what we infer

### R1 · Siena Film Foundation: filmic editorial
- Live: https://siena.film/
- Design interview: https://www.commarts.com/webpicks/siena-film-foundation
- Screenshot reference: https://www.commarts.com/project/37665/siena-film-foundation
- **Reported by designers:** cinematic large imagery, filmstrip-like WebGL project discovery, editorial typography, ambient transitions, and global/local contextual navigation. The team explicitly tried to reduce motion that distracted from the content.
- **Relevant SHOWTIME principle (our interpretation):** the frame/pacing can feel like a screening while real works retain their own audio/visual voice; avoid reproducing their curved film reel, preloader or specific typography.

### R2 · Sculpting Harmony, Getty + Resn: process-driven artistic documentary
- Live: https://gehry.getty.edu/
- Design interview: https://www.commarts.com/webpicks/sculpting-harmony
- **Reported by designers:** Getty's archive of Gehry's Walt Disney Concert Hall integrates sketches, models, music, history and interactive documentary structure; a semilinear framework encourages free depth instead of railroading the visitor. The original object's visual logic (freeform sketches, sound) informed the art direction.
- **Relevant principle:** backstage/process evidence can be aesthetically compelling when it originates in *actual* source materials. For SHOWTIME: radio/cue chronology, script documents, creative research, mechanics diagrams and production notes — where those assets really exist. **Do not fake technical drawings to decorate the page.**

### R3 · Love Letters to Cities, Thyssen Museum + Sayavera/Yinsen: multi-format editorial freedom
- Live: https://www.museothyssen.org/webdocs/conectathyssen/cartas-amor-ciudades/en/
- Studio case: https://sayavera.studio/en/docuweb-love-letters-to-cities/
- Design interview: https://www.commarts.com/webpicks/love-letters-to-cities
- **Verified from sources:** videos, podcasts, articles, images; visitor can follow a suggested path or drift freely. Type and whitespace, day/night and ordered/chaotic modes form an editorial system rather than separate mini-sites.
- **Relevant principle:** SHOWTIME's breadth is manageable if visitor choice is structured without reducing each medium to the same decorative card. Fast route and exploratory route should share one content base.

### R4 · Benno's Light, makemepulse: emotion attached to an interaction with purpose
- Studio case: https://www.makemepulse.com/case-study/bennos-light-claims-conference
- **Reported by studio:** six-chapter interactive testimony; central light interaction serves human narrative, with historical editorial context accessible separately without interrupting it.
- **Relevant principle:** an effect should reveal what a work *means*; light in SHOWTIME can signal the act of presentation without becoming an expensive decorative fireworks system. Do not copy this project's testimony, aesthetic or scene structure.

### R5 · Bruno Simon: when the website itself is the skill demonstration
- Live: https://bruno-simon.com/
- Text version: https://bruno-simon.com/html/
- **Verified:** the site invites visitors to drive around a rendered 3D world and exposes its construction, controls and a separate HTML route.
- **Relevant boundary:** outstanding for a creative developer showing gameplay. SHOWTIME also needs music, video, event evidence and recruiter access; therefore a mandatory drivable space is rejected as its sole discovery method. Optional project-level interactions remain valid.

### R6 · Accessible motion as a production requirement
- W3C: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions
- Web.dev: https://web.dev/learn/accessibility/motion
- W3C advises disabling unnecessary motion from interaction for people with motion sensitivities; motion preference and a user control must override decorative effects. This is a design requirement, not a statement that all motion is harmful.

**Research limit:** These references were read from their live/studio/design descriptions and selected image results. SHOWTIME itself has *not* been visually prototyped or usability tested. Suggested colors, timings and style below are hypotheses to test, not externally verified success metrics.

## 2. Three genuinely different visual territories

### A — BLACK BOX / LIVING STAGE
**Thesis:** `THE SHOW IS THE WORK`. The site feels like a contemporary black-box performance venue whose light changes around genuine artifacts; never a literal 3D theatre you must wander through.

**Emotional signature:** anticipation → impact → intimacy → proof.

**Visual grammar:**
- Black ink / graphite field, restrained cream editorial typography, rich yet subtle contrast.
- One **warm spotlight** accent anchored to the real imagery, not five neon colors competing with photos.
- Edge-to-edge film frames when warranted; photographic stage atmospheres, real image grain *inside source photos*, measured shadows/vignette outside media; avoid Photoshop-style noise/scanline all over text.
- Type is the main stagecraft: a strong, confident expressive headline voice + extremely legible straightforward body and source metadata. Big display titles are for **the work**, not endless slogan billboards.
- Motion is **cue-driven**: a moment of deliberate reveal as a work enters; when watching, the interface quiets down. No perpetual floating 3D objects over content.
- Film/audio/gallery composition is medium-native: 16:9 or true source aspect video, vertical 9:16 TikTok, pixel-crisp game surfaces, music releases with legitimate platform embedding.

**Provisional visual tokens (not approved final colors):**
- Backdrop: `#0C0D10` (near-black graphite).
- Primary copy: `#F2F0E9` (soft stage-ivory).
- Secondary text: `#A6A5A2`; avoid gray below accessible contrast.
- Signature light: warm `#E7A66D` or precise warm white (choose ONE in final).
- Image-driven accent: allow deep violet/magenta from **real** NHP_0440 stage photography; do not artificially recolor all art to purple.

**Proof of fit:** strongest for real singer/performer portraits, stage event photographs, full videos, Que Quá Quao and soundtrack; APEX/Ragnarok retain their native gameplay aesthetics framed by neutral backstage identity. MathPath/EventFlow need a crisp light-toned documentation mode to avoid disappearing into theatre darkness.

**Risk:** generic "luxury black cinematic template", tired spotlight/film-grain cliché, performance content visually overshadowing apps. Countermeasure: very strict typography, rich authentic work surfaces, quieter proof layers and meaningful whitespace. No fake venue/spotlight shots.

**References:** R1 Siena + R4 Benno's Light; R2 for genuine backstage documents.

### B — SIGNAL / CUE ROOM
**Thesis:** A creative production/control instrument whose art direction comes from real timing, sound, cue, test and iteration — as if a production desk had become an editorial language.

**Emotional signature:** craft → exactness → rhythm → confidence.

**Visual grammar:**
- Matte charcoal/steel and chalk/ice paper surfaces; one signal color (coral-orange or process red), not cyberpunk RGB.
- Thin grids, calibrated rules, typographic indices and timecode-like notation *only when representing real timelines*; actual media takes up big clear surfaces without overlays.
- Strong readable grotesk + smaller restrained mono for filenames/timecodes/source IDs. Avoid terminal/coding aesthetic as a substitute for personal creativity.
- UI transitions behave as **state changes / cue calls**, immediate and deterministic; subtle focus/fade rather than cinematic fireworks.
- Work proof feels like a real exhibition catalog: working roles, sources, release/version notes, recorded outcomes.
- Photographic lighting stays native, not all content washed into metallic gray.

**Provisional tokens:** `#14181C` charcoal, `#F0EEE5` paper, `#B5C0C3` secondary, `#FA784E` signal highlight.

**Proof of fit:** event sidestage coordination, EventFlow, MathPath, AI content-system docs, game system experimentation; good for recruiters who value production discipline.

**Risk:** loses humanity, warmth and voice/performance identity; feels like SaaS dashboard, pseudo-technical sci-fi game HUD or project-management tool. Countermeasure: real people and original media always dominate, no decorative fake data, instrumentation only in backstage view.

**References:** R2 Sculpting Harmony (source-specific process grammar) + R3 (editorial indices and alternate paths). This is an **inferred design synthesis**, not a claim that the reference sites look like a control room.

### C — OPEN ATELIER / LIVING INDEX
**Thesis:** A contemporary multimedia art publication: each work has its own surface and color; the framing system is elegant, typographic and neutral.

**Emotional signature:** curiosity → intimacy → discovery → credibility.

**Visual grammar:**
- Warm light paper / pale limestone neutral with black ink typography, generous whitespace, one strong cultural/editorial accent; black-background moments appear only when the media demands it.
- Asymmetric but rational grid; oversized titles used sparingly; juxtaposition of photographic archives, actual game screens and source notes.
- Different media acquire different editorial modes: reading a case is not the same as playing a game or watching a video; still they share title, credit, provenance and return patterns.
- Motion from paper/object/typography emphasis, not noisy sci-fi transitions.
- Source/proof information feels natural like museum labeling, with optional deeper notes.

**Provisional tokens:** `#F3F0E9` warm paper, `#1C1D21` ink, `#75716A` caption, `#435DBB` restrained blue OR `#DB5A43` warm vermilion (one final accent only).

**Proof of fit:** exceptional for research methods, MathPath, EventFlow, thumbnails/film stills, mixed disciplines and long-form curated text; maintains a trustworthy archival feel.

**Risk:** feels like a well-designed generic portfolio rather than a distinct SHOWTIME performance; softens game/VR/VFX energy, could end up like an online magazine. Countermeasure: preserve selected dark cinematic performance chapters, one strong brand signature and temporal pacing.

**References:** R3 Love Letters to Cities, R2 Sculpting Harmony; use authentic editorial systems, not direct copies of their grid/style.

## 5. Art-direction system requirements if A is selected

### Typography roles (not yet choosing fonts)
- **Display:** editorial grotesk or expressive high-contrast face with unmistakable word shapes; `SHOWTIME` must read as authored identity, not “generic SaaS” text.
- **Narrative/body:** neutral legible sans, with tested Vietnamese diacritics in headings, body and italics.
- **Metadata:** secondary mono/narrow face *only* for project role, release status, source proof and cue-like annotations. Never set long Vietnamese paragraphs in monospaced text.
- **Hierarchy:** project title / one-line purpose / actual artifact / direct role / proof. The hero title must not consume 100% of a mobile viewport while real work remains hidden.
- **Legibility gate:** real source images may be complex. Text over media requires intentional contrast and a plain-text fallback, not fragile translucent overlays.

### Material and compositing
- Native media aspect ratio and pixel integrity have priority over decorative frames.
- Motion blur/grain can support transitions around content, never degrade readable screenshots or original photographs.
- Avoid fake film perforations, camera REC timers, sliders, VU meters and event cue data unless their meaning is real.
- Preserve a coherent visual rhythm through spacing, type scales, light levels and project transitions, not the exact same card for every work.

### Motion grammar — proposed design limits (subject to usability and performance tests)
- **Function-first microfeedback:** quick, readable, interruptible transitions; candidate 120–220 ms for small state changes.
- **Entering/leaving a work:** candidate 280–500 ms visual reveal for optional rich mode; no enforced long intro and no animation delaying click-through.
- **Main work open:** surrounding motion should become quiet; the user controls audio and playback; no auto-playing simultaneous media.
- **Reduced motion:** static transitions/short fades and all content paths remain usable; honor `prefers-reduced-motion`; disable nonessential parallax/zoom as W3C guidance suggests.
- **Responsiveness:** first meaningful content must appear without WebGL; any richer render is optional progressive enhancement; low-end mobile is not second-class.
- **No-flash safety:** no rapid strobing lighting design merely because “SHOWTIME” suggests a concert.

### Copy tone
- Confident, clear and human. Specific source-backed roles/results beat vague adjectives like “visionary”, “revolutionary”, “next-gen creator”.
- When the user is a vibecoder, name AI-assisted toolmaking and creative direction as such; source code volume is not equal to hand-written programming expertise.
- Product labels: `Play Demo`, `Listen`, `Watch Full Film`, `Explore Process`, `Source`; these are semantic intent examples, not UI components to build.

## 6. Art direction anti-patterns — do not accept these in future production

- **Generic gaming HUD:** electric purple/cyan glow, sci-fi fonts, fake enemy health bars, glass cards, hexagons and particles everywhere; would trivialize event/music/editorial content.
- **Luxury black template:** autoplay video wash, wafer-thin unreadable typography, endless 3D zoom/loader, empty slogans masking missing work.
- **Museum-white template:** all work reduced to squares in a portfolio gallery, generic smooth scroll and endless case-study grids; loses the SHOWTIME artistic identity.
- **Literal cinema corridor:** forced 3D theatre hallway to reach content; awkward on mobile and displaces playable/artifacts.
- **Mixed-style chaos:** pixel game VFX, music cover gradients, audio spectrum and EventFlow dashboard all competing in one scene.
- **Fake proof production:** fabricated backstage images, cinematic screenshots, fake contest badges, fake commercial metrics, unlicensed clips or shortened footage sold as the original.
- **Poor Vietnamese typography:** glyph fallback, missing marks, uncomfortable tracking of uppercase accents.

## 7. Quality-gate checklist for the later designer / implementer

1. Site reads as **SHOWTIME**, not “a game landing page” or “a film company template”.
2. User can identify creator role and open meaningful original work in 5–30 seconds; theatrical motion does not block access.
3. The project media remains native, readable and full-length when the user wants it. One original source can be opened directly.
4. A recruiter can skim without audio, with weak hardware or reduced motion.
5. Live photos convey real performance presence; application/game imagery isn't fake, outdated or artificially misrepresented.
6. Frontstage/backstage/proof distinction survives on mobile and without hover.
7. Actual source links and statuses (published, demo, active development, historical proof) cannot be visually confused.
8. Rhythm is deliberate: one expressive focus at a time, not five animated panels.
9. A gallery fallback is still premium even if all WebGL animation is off.
10. Type and contrast are tested in Vietnamese with real long project titles.
11. One coherent visual language contains *diverse native project languages* without enforcing recoloring.
12. No copy or reinterpretation of references' protected visual assets/components; research images are inspiration only.

## 8. Decision to request from owner (ONLY one decision now)

Choose the **base emotional art territory**, not the UI implementation:
- **A — BLACK BOX / LIVING STAGE:** cinematic, dramatic, authentic performer/production energy. **Recommended.**
- **B — SIGNAL / CUE ROOM:** sharp, precise, production-centric/technical, less emotional.
- **C — OPEN ATELIER / LIVING INDEX:** experimental editorial, museum-grade, cultural, less overtly theatrical.

A valid response can also be “A but warmer,” “A with more chaotic edge,” “C but with darker stage moments,” etc. Those are creative-direction decisions.

**If A receives approval**, next step is **visual identity design brief** (wordmark direction, typography/palette tests, real-asset moodboard boundaries, visual quality criteria and budget), still without building HTML or deploying a page. If A is rejected, revise the art direction and references before design.
